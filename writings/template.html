A clean title in italic

A short subtitle that frames the piece in one breath — the question, the angle, or the conclusion in miniature.

Open with the strongest sentence you have. This template auto-renders a terracotta drop-cap on the first letter of the first paragraph, so the first word matters more than the rest of the sentence. Keep the opening grounded in a moment, an observation, or a tension — not a thesis statement.

Then give yourself room to think on the page. A second paragraph is where most pieces start to find their footing. You can use bold for emphasis, italics for asides, or link out to people, sources, or earlier writing.

A section break looks like this

Use H2s sparingly — maybe two or three across a 1,000-word piece. They work best when they're declarative rather than label-like (good: "Why I stopped tracking everything"; less good: "Methodology"). H2s support the same italic-terracotta accent as the title.

Smaller sub-label

For finer subdivisions within a section, use the small caps H3 label. Keep these short — three or four words.

Use pull-quotes to slow the reader down. They work best for sentences that earn the pause — your own line you want to draw attention to, or someone else's that you're responding to. — Optional attribution

Lists work when the underlying thought is genuinely list-shaped:

  • Each item should be self-contained.
  • Parallel structure helps — items that share a grammatical shape read faster.
  • Three to five items is the sweet spot; more than that, prose usually serves better.

The thin centered divider above signals a soft section change — useful for moving between scenes without a hard heading.

Close with the line you've been circling the whole piece. The last sentence is the one readers carry with them; treat it as the second-most-important sentence after the opener.
